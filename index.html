FIELD NOTES ON INFORMATION CULTURE Nº 001

A short field guide to paying attention

Information
culture is a
way of seeing.

gather, question, share — in that order.

FOUR HABITS · ONE EXERCISE · THREE MINUTES · ZERO ALGORITHMS

01WHAT IT MEANS

Information culture is not about knowing more things. It is the quiet craft of deciding what deserves your attention — checking where a claim was born, asking what it leaves out, and passing on only what survives the questions.

information culture /n./ — the habits, institutions and small courtesies that turn raw signal into shared knowledge.

It lives in libraries and newsrooms, in footnotes and citations — and in the two seconds you pause before pressing share.

02FOUR HABITS
  1. 01

    Source before story

    Trace the origin first. A claim without an origin is a rumour wearing a good font.

  2. 02

    Context is content

    The same fact can warn or reassure depending on what stands beside it. Read the surroundings, not just the sentence.

  3. 03

    Share slower than you scroll

    Reposting is publishing. The pause between reading and forwarding is where culture actually happens.

  4. 04

    Keep the archive warm

    Knowledge survives by being stored, cited and revisited. Old information is not dead information — it is foundation.
